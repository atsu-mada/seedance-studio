Reference Workflow

Asset Role Map

Before writing prompt prose, assign every uploaded asset a role. Role mapping prevents accidental transfer of identity, logos, scene ownership, or incompatible camera and motion instructions.

AssetGood RolesAvoid
Imageidentity, product, pose, costume, environment, first frame, last frameasking it to define unseen motion
Videomotion, camera, pacing, blocking, timing, gesture rhythmcopying protected identity, logo, or scene ownership
Audiorhythm, tempo, mood, ambience, delivery tone, music textureassuming voice, song, or likeness authorization
Storyboardrough blocking, subject placement, camera flowidentity, costume, brand, dialogue, audio, lighting, environment detail, final style, start frame
Text briefaction, genre, camera plan, constraintsreplacing concrete reference roles with vague mood words

Rules

  • Preserve reference tags exactly.
  • Give every reference one primary role before writing style language.
  • Do not ask one reference to control incompatible roles unless the tradeoff is explicit.
  • Use owned, licensed, public-domain, or clearly authorized references.
  • Write what should transfer and what should not transfer.
  • When authorization is unclear, transfer broad motion, tempo, mood, or production function rather than protected identity.
  • Treat multimodal reference generation, video edit, video extend, and first/last-frame generation as separate tasks. They can share assets, but the prompt should name the active workflow.
  • If audio and video references compete, make the video silent when audio timing must dominate, or state that the video controls camera/motion only and [Audio1] controls tempo.
  • In sequences, separate canonical references from accepted continuity sources: canonical identity/product references control immutable design, while accepted previous footage controls transient opening state.
  • Never let a motion reference overwrite continuity locks, completed beats, reserved beats, or exact reference tags.
  • Canonical identity and accepted continuity state always override storyboard. Do not allow storyboard-derived identity, costume, brand, dialogue, audio, lighting, environment detail, or style into final prompt/state.

Storyboard Reference Workflow

Use this workflow only when the input is a clip beat, canonical character/environment references, and existing composition requirements. Generate storyboard candidates outside active references and outside canonical state. The candidate board is a planning artifact, not a source frame or style reference.

Candidate-board style: white or pale paper; black-gray rough line art; GridTileStyle; readable character shape, orientation, and action; sparse backgrounds. Panel numbers and transition arrows are permitted in boards. Do not use captions, logos, watermarks, color fills, finished illustration, or strong shading.

Storyboard controls rough blocking, subject placement, and camera flow only. Use this exact sending phrase:

storyboard controls rough blocking and camera flow only; do not transfer start frame, final visual style, lighting, character identity, costume, or environment detail.

Source-panel numbers and transition arrows are planning annotations only and must not transfer to generated video.

Candidate Protocol

For every candidate, record: candidate ID, batch ID, inputs, prompt version, dimensions, hash, provenance status, authorization status, risk status, reviewer result, selection status, and approval status. Candidate states are exactly isolated, pending_approval, approved, and rejected.

  1. Create candidates in isolated state; keep active references, canonical state, final prompts, and parent sources unchanged.
  2. Batch-review every candidate with a human. If any required provenance or authorization is missing, stop before human approval.
  3. Mark the reviewed batch pending_approval until explicit batch approval records approver, timestamp, and selected candidate IDs.
  4. Only selected approved candidates may be mapped into a Seedance send. Before that approval, active references, canonical state, final prompts, and parent sources remain unchanged. Pending or rejected candidates cannot progress.
  5. For failed candidate regeneration, choose exactly one reason: finished-look, too-rough, unclear-action, unclear-layout, or unwanted-text. Preserve active assets while regenerating.

Canonical identity and accepted continuity state always override storyboard. Final prompts and state must exclude storyboard-derived identity, costume, brand, dialogue, audio, lighting, environment detail, and style.

Approved Send Pattern

After explicit batch approval only, send the approved storyboard with canonical references and accepted continuity state, preserving role separation:

[Storyboard1] storyboard controls rough blocking and camera flow only; do not transfer start frame, final visual style, lighting, character identity, costume, or environment detail. Canonical references control identity and environment; accepted continuity state controls the opening state. Do not render source-panel numbers or transition arrows in generated video.

Motion Transfer

Field-observed technique; test before promising results. Probably the most under-used reference capability: a donor video drives choreography or camera rhythm while an image keeps identity.

  • Pair one donor [Video1] with one identity anchor [Image1], and write the exclusion explicitly: [Video1] controls the choreography only - nothing of its appearance, performer, costume, room, or logo transfers.
  • Pick donor clips with one clear action, a clean silhouette, and a steady camera. Busy multi-person footage transfers noise, not motion.
  • Mute the donor clip before upload unless its sound should drive timing; if it keeps sound, state which reference owns the clock.
  • Transfers well: choreography, gesture timing, camera rhythm, blocking. Transfers poorly: fine hand detail, multi-person sync, facial performance.
  • Use only owned, licensed, stock, mocap, rehearsal, or self-recorded donor footage; real-person donors transfer general motion only, never likeness.

Template

[Image1] controls product identity. [Video1] controls camera pace only. [Audio1] controls tempo only. Preserve the subject from [Image1]; do not copy characters, logos, music, voice, or environment from [Video1]/[Audio1].

Sequence Transfer Template

[Video 1] is the accepted previous clip and controls only the actual opening state, camera phase, motion phase, ambience, and environment arrangement. @Image 1 controls canonical identity. Preserve both tags exactly. Do not copy unrelated identity, costume, logo, or future action from any reference.

Previz-Driven R2V Prompt Structure [field]

Field-observed structure (2026-09) for reference-to-video jobs driven by a block previz video. It keeps the previz in its one role and gives the model timed events with end states. Use it only in R2V mode; on surfaces where first/last-frame keyframes are exclusive with references, a keyframe job cannot carry the previz.

  • [参照の役割] (reference roles): one line per reference. @Video 1 is the previz and carries only camera path, timing, and subject positions, never colors, shapes, boxes, spheres, or lighting. Identity and look come from image references or text.
  • [狙い] (intent): one or two sentences on what the shot is for and what the viewer should feel.
  • [出来事] (events): contiguous time ranges that add up to the job duration. Each range has one action and an explicit end state (終了状態) that the next range starts from.
  • [固定] (locks): what must not change: real forms of previz proxies (see proxy-shape transfer), identity, materials, no on-screen text, cut or no-cut.
  • Preserve the reference tags exactly as the active surface shows them.
[参照の役割]
@Video 1 はプレビズ。カメラの動き・タイミング・被写体の位置だけを参照し、色・形・箱や球などの仮形状・ライティングは参照しない。
@Image 1 は少女の外見（顔・髪・服）を固定する。
@Image 2 は紙細工の町の美術と質感を決める。

[狙い]
朝の紙細工の町。少女が放った紙飛行機を追い、町の広さに気づく瞬間を描く。

[出来事]
0–4秒：屋根の上の少女が紙飛行機を放つ。終了状態：飛行機は画面右上へ離れ、少女は見上げている。
4–9秒：カメラは飛行機を追って上昇し、町全体が見える高さまで引く。終了状態：町の全景、飛行機は中央の小さな点。
9–12秒：飛行機が朝日を横切る。終了状態：逆光の飛行機のシルエットがほぼ静止している。

[固定]
少女は人の形のまま（光る球にしない）。町は紙の質感。文字・ロゴなし。カットなしの1回の連続した動き。

継承属性と競合仮説

参照ごとに、継承する人物同一性・場所の形状・効果の接続点と、継承しない背景・画角・状態を明記する。全カテゴリを毎ショットへ機械的に添付しない。競合は観測した症状と仮説を分け、一変数の比較で調べる。
